# Aircraft Rivets & Rivet Nuts: Aviation Fastening Solutions for Aircraft Structures

Aircraft rivets and rivet nuts are important fastening components used across airframe structures, panels, fairings, interior assemblies, and equipment installations. Rivets provide a lightweight method of permanently joining materials, while rivet nuts provide an internally threaded fastening point where a removable screw or bolt is required. Their applications can range from structural sheet-metal work to frequently serviced aircraft panels and fittings.

Riveting remains an important aircraft construction and maintenance technique because riveted joints can provide effective load transfer while adding relatively little weight. Solid rivets are widely associated with metal aircraft structures, while specialized blind and structural rivets can be used where access to the rear side of a joint is limited. Rivet nuts serve a different purpose by creating a threaded attachment point in sheet material or assemblies where conventional nuts may be difficult to access.

## Solid Rivets for Aircraft Structural Assemblies

Solid rivets are widely used in aircraft structures because they provide permanent fastening for sheet-metal and structural assemblies. Applications can include fuselage skins, wing components, ribs, spars, fittings, brackets, and other airframe structures. A solid rivet consists of a manufactured head and shank, with the opposite end formed during installation to create the completed joint.

The selection of a solid rivet depends on several factors, including the material being joined, rivet material, shank diameter, length, head configuration, and the requirements of the specific aircraft structure. Common aircraft rivet designs can include universal-head and countersunk configurations, with different head styles selected according to the surrounding structure and surface requirements.

## Blind Rivets for Limited-Access Applications

Blind rivets are designed for installations where access to both sides of the joint is restricted. Unlike conventional solid rivets that require access to the opposite side for driving or squeezing, blind rivets can be installed from one side using an appropriate installation tool. This characteristic makes them useful for selected applications involving enclosed structures, panels, fairings, and other areas where rear access is limited.

Not every blind rivet is appropriate for aircraft use. Different blind rivets can have different load capabilities, materials, locking arrangements, and installation characteristics. Aviation maintenance documentation should therefore be used to determine whether a particular blind rivet is approved for the intended application rather than substituting a general-purpose commercial rivet.

## Aircraft Rivet Nuts for Removable Assemblies

Rivet nuts, sometimes called threaded rivet nuts, provide an internally threaded attachment point in sheet material and other relatively thin structures. They can be particularly useful where an assembly needs to accept a screw or bolt but there is insufficient access to hold a conventional nut behind the material. Once installed, the rivet nut remains attached to the parent material and provides a threaded connection for a removable component.

Aircraft applications for rivet nuts can include access covers, fairings, trim panels, interior components, equipment installations, and other lightly loaded fittings that require repeated removal and installation. Rivet nuts are available in different configurations, including flat-head and countersunk styles, with selection influenced by the parent material thickness, required screw size, and desired surface configuration.

## Rivet Materials, Head Styles, and Dimensions

Aircraft rivets are available in different materials selected according to the application and the materials being joined. Aluminum alloy rivets are commonly associated with aircraft sheet-metal construction, while other rivet materials may be specified for applications requiring different mechanical or environmental characteristics. Material compatibility is important because an inappropriate combination can contribute to corrosion or other installation concerns.

Head configuration is another important consideration. Universal-head rivets can provide a protruding head, while countersunk rivets can provide a flush surface where aerodynamic or clearance requirements make a protruding head unsuitable. Rivet diameter and length also need to correspond with the joint dimensions and installation requirements.

## Aircraft Rivets for MRO and Structural Repair

Rivet replacement is a common consideration during aircraft inspection, structural maintenance, refurbishment, and repair activities. A damaged, missing, or improperly installed rivet may require evaluation and replacement according to approved maintenance or structural repair information. The replacement process can involve identifying the existing hardware, evaluating the joint, preparing the hole as required, and selecting the appropriate replacement fastener.

The correct rivet length and diameter are particularly important because the completed rivet must form an appropriate shop head and establish the intended joint characteristics. Hole condition can also affect the completed installation. For this reason, aircraft maintenance personnel generally rely on approved maintenance information rather than treating rivet replacement as a generic hardware substitution.

Aircraft rivets and rivet nuts therefore serve different but complementary functions across aviation assemblies. Solid and specialized rivets provide permanent fastening for many structural applications, while rivet nuts provide removable threaded attachment points in selected panels and fittings. For aircraft parts distributors, MRO organizations, and procurement teams, accurate identification and specification matching remain central to sourcing the correct aviation fastening components.
